\section{Existing Methodologies}
Traditional corporate benchmarking has evolved from manual spreadsheet analysis to proprietary financial databases. While foundational ratio analysis \cite{altman1968financial} and valuation frameworks \cite{damodaran2012investment} established standard metrics, manual transcription remains slow, labor-intensive, and prone to error. Conversely, commercial financial terminals automate data harvesting across public filings; however, closed database architectures and prohibitive annual licensing fees (\$24,000+ per workstation) prevent independent researchers from deploying customized statistical screening pipelines.

\section{Machine Learning \& Robust Outlier Detection in Finance}

Because corporate accounting distributions exhibit heavy tails and skewness, robust non-parametric statistics and unsupervised machine learning algorithms are deployed to isolate empirical anomalies (Figure~\ref{fig:lit_workflow}).

\subsection{Tukey's Fences (IQR Outlier Detection)}
Tukey's Fences provide a non-parametric outlier boundary relying on order statistics rather than arithmetic moments \cite{tukey1977exploratory}. The Interquartile Range ($\text{IQR}$) measures the statistical dispersion of the central $50\%$ of the corporate peer group:
\begin{equation}
    \text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1
\end{equation}
where $Q_1$ and $Q_3$ denote the first and third quartiles ($25^{\text{th}}$ and $75^{\text{th}}$ percentiles). Tukey Fences establish operational inlier boundaries using a scaling multiplier $k$ ($1.50$):
\begin{align}
    \text{Lower Bound} &= Q_1 - k \times \text{IQR} \\
    \text{Upper Bound} &= Q_3 + k \times \text{IQR}
\end{align}
Observations outside $[\text{Lower Bound}, \; \text{Upper Bound}]$ are flagged as empirical univariate outliers, ensuring complete invariance to heavy-tailed distributional skewness.

\subsection{Robust Modified Z-Score (MAD)}
While traditional Z-scores normalize deviations by the standard deviation, a single extreme outlier inflates the denominator and masks adjacent anomalies. To provide robust standardized scaling, the Modified Z-Score utilizes the median and Median Absolute Deviation ($\text{MAD}$) \cite{iglewicz1993how}:
\begin{equation}
    \text{MAD} = \text{median}\left( |x_i - \tilde{x}| \right)
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{x}$ represents the peer median. The robust Modified Z-Score ($M_i$) for company $i$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    M_i = 0.6745 \times \frac{x_i - \tilde{x}}{\text{MAD}}
\end{equation}
The consistency factor $0.6745$ ensures asymptotic equivalence to standard normal Z-scores for Gaussian distributions. Observations with $|M_i| > 3.50$ are flagged as severe distributional deviations.

\subsection{Isolation Forest (Multivariate Anomaly Detection)}
Univariate filters screen ratios independently; however, reporting irregularities frequently manifest as joint structural anomalies across interconnected pillars. The Isolation Forest isolates anomalies by recursively partitioning feature spaces \cite{liu2008isolation}. The anomaly score $s(x, n)$ across an ensemble of trees is:
\begin{equation}
    s(x, n) = 2^{-\frac{E(h(x))}{c(n)}}
\end{equation}
where $h(x)$ is path length, $E(h(x))$ is expected path length, and $c(n)$ is average path length:
\begin{equation}
    c(n) = 2\ln(n - 1) + 0.5772156649 - \frac{2(n - 1)}{n}
\end{equation}
Observations with $s(x, n) \ge 0.50$ represent multivariate structural anomalies (Figure~\ref{fig:outlier_cluster_pipeline}).

\section{Research Gaps}
A critical review of the literature reveals four fundamental research gaps in financial benchmarking:
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength{\topsep}{1pt}
    \setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
    \setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Nomenclature Disparities}: Divergent line-item terminology across accounting standards (Ind-AS, IFRS, US-GAAP) leads to schema mismatches and unhandled nulls in rigid scrapers.
    \item \textbf{Baseline Outlier Contamination}: Prevailing models compute peer baselines directly over unscrubbed data, allowing extreme non-recurring charges to distort arithmetic averages.
    \item \textbf{Multivariate Anomaly Gaps}: Traditional tools screen ratios independently, missing multi-dimensional accounting irregularities that span interconnected operational pillars.
    \item \textbf{Categorical Scoring Deficits}: Conventional scorecards rely on arbitrary discrete letter grades rather than continuous, distribution-preserving sigmoid mapping functions.
\end{enumerate}

The \textit{FinMetrics Suite} overcomes these deficiencies through automated synonym mapping, three-tier outlier filtering, multivariate anomaly isolation, and calibrated sigmoid scoring.
